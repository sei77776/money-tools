# Vendor / MSA Review (Buy Side)

Reviewing as the **customer**. The vendor drafted this paper; assume it is
written for them and read accordingly.

> **Not legal advice.** Material commitments require review by a qualified
> lawyer. Include this in the deliverable.

## Step 1 — Establish the commercial frame

- Annual and total contract value; term length.
- What breaks if this vendor disappears tomorrow? (Determines how hard exit rights matter.)
- Does the vendor touch customer personal data, production systems, or credentials?
- Are we replacing an incumbent, and is there a migration dependency?

## Step 2 — Work the buy-side checklist

**Money**
- Price, uplift cap on renewal (**an uncapped renewal uplift is a top-three finding** — push for CPI or a fixed %)
- Overage/usage pricing and how it is measured
- Payment terms and whether prepayment is required
- True-up mechanics; can seat count go down, or only up?

**Term & exit** — the clauses vendors rely on you not reading
- **Auto-renewal and the notice window.** Note the exact deadline date and recommend a calendar reminder 30 days before it. A missed window locks in another full term.
- Termination for convenience — do we have it? Vendors often reserve it only for themselves.
- Termination for cause, cure periods.
- **Exit assistance**: data export in a usable format, retention window after termination, deletion certification. Without this, exit is theoretical.

**Service quality**
- SLA: uptime %, how measured, exclusions (planned maintenance often makes the number meaningless — read the exclusions).
- Remedy for breach — service credits only? Credits are usually the sole remedy; check whether repeated breach gives a termination right, and ask for one if not.
- Support response times by severity.

**Data & security**
- DPA present and adequate; sub-processor list and notice of changes.
- Data location and cross-border transfer mechanism.
- Security commitments — certifications, breach notification timeline to us.
- Our ownership of our data, stated explicitly.
- Rights to use our data for the vendor's model training or product improvement — **check for this specifically**; it is frequently buried in an acceptable-use or privacy addendum.

**Risk**
- Liability cap — is it mutual, and is it a meaningful multiple of fees? A cap at "fees paid in the prior 3 months" is common and often too low for a critical vendor.
- Vendor indemnity for IP infringement and data breach.
- Insurance evidence.

**Documents incorporated by reference** — online ToS, AUP, SLA pages the vendor can change unilaterally. Read them. If they were not provided, the review is incomplete; say so.

## Step 3 — Rank and draft asks

Blocker / High / Medium / Low, each with proposed language and a fallback
position. Identify which 2–3 asks matter most — a request list of 20 items gets
refused wholesale; a focused one gets agreed.

## Step 4 — Verify

- Did you locate and read every incorporated document?
- Did you record the exact auto-renewal notice deadline as a date?
- Did you check unilateral-change rights over pricing and terms?
- Did you confirm what happens to our data 30/90 days after termination?

## Step 5 — Deliver

Bottom line (sign / negotiate / walk), ranked findings with redlines, a "key
dates" box (renewal notice deadline, term end), and the top 3 negotiation asks.
