# Hiring & Onboarding

## Step 1 — Define the role before writing anything public

Answer these; a posting written before them attracts the wrong people:

- What outcomes must this person deliver in 12 months? (Outcomes, not duties.)
- What does the first 90 days produce?
- Which existing work moves off someone else's plate — and is it documented? (If not, run `sop-writer` first; onboarding into undocumented work is the main reason new hires stall.)
- Must-have vs nice-to-have skills. Keep must-haves to 3–5; longer lists shrink the pool without improving the hire.
- Level, salary band, and employment type — decide the band **before** interviewing, not after meeting someone.

## Step 2 — Write the posting

Structure: what the company does and why it matters → what this person will
achieve → what a typical week looks like → what's required → what's offered
(publish the salary range) → how to apply and what the process is.

Avoid: "rockstar/ninja", unpaid test projects of more than ~2 hours, an
open-ended list of 15 requirements, and requirements that are proxies for
demographics rather than for the job.

## Step 3 — Run a structured process

Structured beats unstructured on both prediction and fairness. Fix these in advance:

- The same core questions for every candidate, in the same order.
- A **scorecard** per interview: which specific competencies this stage assesses, scored 1–4 with written evidence for each score.
- Work-sample task that resembles the actual job, time-boxed and paid if substantial.
- Interviewers score independently **before** discussing — group discussion first collapses independent signal into the loudest opinion.
- Reference checks with specific behavioral questions, not "would you rehire".

Ask behavioral questions ("tell me about a time you...") and probe for specifics.
Vague answers to specific probes are themselves signal.

Do not ask about age, family status, nationality, health, religion, or protected
characteristics; in many jurisdictions salary history is also off-limits. Flag
any such question if it appears in a draft.

## Step 4 — Onboarding plan

**Before day 1:** contract signed, equipment ordered, accounts created (list them),
payroll set up, first-day schedule sent, buddy assigned.

**Week 1:** access verified working, meet the team, read the key docs, ship one
small real thing. Shipping something tiny in week 1 does more for confidence than
any amount of orientation.

**30 / 60 / 90 days:** written goals for each checkpoint, agreed with the person,
with a scheduled review at each.

## Step 5 — Verify

- Is the salary band set and the posting range consistent with it?
- Does every interview stage have a defined competency and scorecard? Stages without them are unstructured chat.
- Are all pre-day-1 items assigned to a named owner with a date?
- Are the 30/60/90 goals observable, not aspirational?

## Step 6 — Deliver

Role definition, job posting, interview guide with scorecards, structured
process timeline, and the onboarding checklist with owners and dates.
